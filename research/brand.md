# Brand Language & Visual Identity Audit — Motiq

**Important framing:** Motiq has never had a website. So there is no "old website styling" to separate out. Everything recorded here comes from the brand's own signage, packaging, photography and social output, which means **all of it is core brand material.** Nothing below is legacy web decoration that can be discarded.

---

| Element | What it is | Where seen |

The period after the name is the one piece of deliberate typographic personality in the identity. It makes the name read as a statement rather than a label.

## B. VERIFIED — Colour, as it actually appears in brand assets

The brand does not publish a palette. These are read off the photography.

**Dark world** (espresso, hojicha, cream tops, food, interior)
- Near-black / soft black backgrounds, slightly warm rather than neutral
- Dark chocolate and roasted brown mid-tones
- Warm amber and caramel as the only bright value — crema, espresso stream, warm downlight
- Dark green-grey marble as a surface
- One recurring accident worth noting: a deep teal velvet chair, the only cool colour anywhere in the dark set

**Light world** (matcha)
- Cream, warm off-white, pale grey backgrounds
- Milk white
- **One saturated, unmixed matcha green** carrying the entire frame

**The through-line:** Motiq never uses more than one strong colour per image. Everything else is a neutral. That restraint is the identity.

## C. VERIFIED — Typography, as used by the brand itself

Seen in their own editorial cards:
- **Display:** a high-contrast serif with fine hairlines and sharp, slightly flared terminals, used in caps and generously letterspaced — "01 BRIGHT", "02 BOLD". Editorial, closer to a fashion masthead than to a coffee-shop sign.
- **Labels:** a clean grotesque sans in caps, small, widely letterspaced, at low opacity — "SIGNATURE ESPRESSO", "NOTES", "ORIGIN", "ROAST", "PROCESS".
- **Body:** the same grotesque at a small size, generous line height, sometimes justified.
- **Logo:** brush script, used only as the logo. Never as headline type.

So the existing relationship is already **script logo → high-contrast serif display → quiet letterspaced sans**. That is a real, working three-part system the brand invented for itself. Phase 1 should build on it rather than replace it.

The exact typefaces are unknown. Fonts with the right traits can be matched; the actual files should be requested from the owner.

## D. VERIFIED — Photographic direction

Two consistent treatments, applied by subject, never mixed:

1. **Dark, single-source, high-drama.** One warm light, deep falloff, long hard shadows, black or dark-wood grounds. Used for espresso, hojicha, food and the room. Product sits small in a large dark frame.
2. **Light, diffused, high-key.** Cream and white grounds, soft shadows, no visible light source. Used for matcha. The green is the only event in the frame.

Shared across both: portrait orientation, tight product framing, no clutter, no props beyond the vessel, no hands except where hands serve the shot, **no faces.** The one person in the set is mid-movement and turned away.

This is the direct basis for the client's instruction to keep each section's background relevant to its picture. The assets are already sorted into two background families; the site should honour that rather than flatten it.

## E. VERIFIED — Physical and packaging
- **Signage:** black script wordmark on a white/light fascia, above a glazed shopfront. Confirmed by exterior photography.
- **Packaging:** clear plastic cold cup with a white circular label carrying the wordmark; clear domed lid. Kraft paper bean bag.
- **Interior:** dark timber wall panelling, fluted pale-wood counter face, glass pastry vitrines, warm pendant and track lighting in the evening. Separately, customer photography of the same space in daylight shows a much brighter room — pale floors, ring pendants, floor-to-ceiling glass, plants, large framed pop art. **The room reads completely differently by day and by night, and we only hold night.**
- **Uniform:** one staff member seen in plain black. Not enough evidence to call it a uniform.

## F. VERIFIED — Brand voice

Recurring vocabulary, pulled from their own captions and menu descriptions:
*whisked, roasted, velvety, smooth, nutty, warm, rich, creamy, silky, balanced, house-made, house-fermented, ceremonial, signature, finished with*

Sentence shape: one short declarative sentence naming the ingredient, then a second clause naming the sensation. Then, often, a bracketed practicality — *(Iced only)*, *(12oz)*, *(Only comes with Oat Milk)*.

Structural habits:
- Numbered series: **01 BRIGHT**, **02 BOLD**
- Three-word rhythms: **BALANCED • SWEET • BRIGHT**, **RICH • DEEP • SMOOTH**, **Coffee • Matcha • Brunch**
- Origin named as a place, set apart: **(Uji) … Kyoto**

**Voice traits, described concretely:**
- **Precise** — origin, roast level and process are published for a cafe, not a roastery.
- **Sensory rather than emotional** — it describes what the drink does in the mouth, not how you will feel about your day.
- **Restrained** — no exclamation marks, no slang, no hype words, no emoji in the menu copy.
- **Quietly proud** — "signature", "ceremonial", "house-made" appear often; "best", "award-winning" and "famous" never appear.
- **Practical when it matters** — hours, parking and cup size are stated plainly right next to the poetry.
- **Not overtly Korean-facing in tone**, though the products are unmistakably Korean-American: misugaru, kimchi carbonara, spam fried rice. The food does the cultural talking; the copy stays neutral.

**Calls to action currently in use:** "Order Online" (Instagram). That is the only CTA the brand has ever written.

**Slogan / tagline:** none exists. "Coffee • Matcha • Brunch" is the closest thing, and it is a category list rather than a slogan. The nearest thing to a brand idea in the owner's own words is the old line **"your second home during the day."**

## G. Where the brand is inconsistent (facts, not criticism)
1. The **espresso bean naming disagrees with itself.** The Instagram cards say *01 Bright* (Brazil/Colombia, medium) and *02 Bold* (Brazil, medium-dark). The Yelp menu says *Monarch* (Colombia + Ethiopia, medium) and *High Limit* (Brazil + Colombia, medium-dark). Two different naming systems for what appears to be the same two-bean programme.
2. The **Kimchi Fried Rice description differs** between the Instagram card ("…and a fresh truffle arugula salad on the side") and the Yelp menu ("…topped with a sunny-side-up egg and spring onions").
3. The **Yelp About text is still the previous brand's**, naming M CAFÉ twice.
4. The **daylight room and the night room** look like two different businesses, and only one of them exists in our asset folder.

None of these are resolvable without the owner. All are listed in `owner-confirmation.md`.
