## 4. DESIGN OBSERVATION

Observations of what exists. Not proposals.

- **The wordmark is script, not sans.** The fascia lettering "motiq." is a black handwritten/brush script, lowercase, terminated with a period. The period is doing real work in the mark. Any type system for the site has to sit under a script logo without fighting it.
- **The interior is bright, modern and gallery-like**, not the dark-wood third-wave idiom: pale terrazzo-style floors, white and light-wood counters, circular ring pendant lights, floor-to-ceiling glass onto Olympic Blvd, plants, and large framed pop-art pieces on white walls. Photographically, the room is high-key and low-contrast.
- **The space is the product as much as the coffee is.** Business-uploaded video captions choose "Lots of space to sit". Yelp's "People also searched for" module surfaces *Study Cafe*, *Study Spots*, *Study*. Reviewer after reviewer describes working, studying, indoor plus a large covered patio with fans, plus a rooftop level. The single most repeated praise across reviews is space and seating, ahead of any drink.
- **"Cream top" is the visual signature and it is inherently cinematic.** Five menu items are built on it (Motiq Cream Top, Orange Zest, Misugaru, Matcha X Matcha, Strawberry, Mango). The cream layer sitting on the drink and then melting through is a pour/layer/dissolve moment — the strongest natural candidate for the site's hero motion. Customers photograph it: Drink is the largest photo category at 147 images.
- **The menu is genuinely two businesses.** A serious drink program ($4.50–$7.50) and a full brunch/lunch kitchen ($7–$17, including kimchi carbonara, kimchi fried rice, a burger and three sandwiches). Most cafe sites would under-serve the food. The review evidence says the food is drawing its own visits.
- **Korean-American specificity is the differentiator, and it is in the product, not the decor.** Misugaru, Kyoto matcha, hojicha with chestnut cream, kimchi carbonara, spam fried rice. The interior itself is brand-neutral modern.
- **The digital presence is thin and inconsistent.** No website. The owner's own Yelp description still carries the previous brand's name. Directories disagree on hours and parking. Third parties (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Apple, magicpin) are each holding a slightly different version of the business. There is currently no source of truth that Motiq controls.
- **Photography risk is the defining constraint of this project.** 457 images exist and effectively none of them are licensable to us. Everything visual is either owner-supplied, newly shot, or newly generated.

## 5. RECOMMENDATION

Recommendations only. Nothing here is decided, and none of it is design work.

1. **Treat the site as Motiq's first source of truth.** Its highest-value job is to be the one place where the name, hours, address, parking, menu and ordering links are correct. Everything else is built on top of that.
2. **Ask the owner for the primary goal before Phase 1.** The three plausible goals lead to materially different sites: (a) drive visits and correct the record, (b) drive online orders through DoorDash/Uber Eats, (c) open catering as a lead channel. The user left PRIMARY GOAL blank. This should be answered, not assumed.
3. **Run an owner intake on the 14 open items in section 2** before any layout work. The fastest version is a single form covering: hours, parking, suite number, current menu export, modifier prices, catering details, the LA Times link, ownership wording, Instagram rights, and domain control.
4. **Commission or supply real photography.** Recommended minimum shot list, all owner-owned: the cream-top pour in motion (hero candidate), 3–4 hero drinks straight-on, 4–6 food plates, the counter/pastry case, the patio in afternoon light, the rooftop level, the room empty and the room full, and the signage. If a shoot is not possible, the fallback is owner-supplied Instagram originals plus newly generated imagery, disclosed as such. Yelp photos are off limits either way.
5. **Fix the platform record in parallel with the build.** Update the Yelp About text away from "M CAFÉ", reconcile the Apple suite number, and align hours across Yelp, Apple, Google, DoorDash and Uber Eats. This costs nothing and it protects the site's credibility.
6. **Do not publish the Los Angeles Times line without the article link.** If the owner supplies it, it becomes a strong, citable proof element. If they cannot, it comes out entirely.
7. **Keep the "study and work" reality honest.** If outlets are scarce and seating fills at peak, say the useful true thing (patio, fans, Wi-Fi, hours until 10 PM) rather than promising a co-working space.
8. **Plan the menu as maintainable content, not baked-in markup.** Prices moved recently enough that the French Toast entry is literally marked "UPDATED". Whatever the build is, menu data should live in one editable file.

### RIGHTS — read this before any image ships
These photos belong to Motiq. We are building on spec without owner contact, so **we do not currently hold a licence to republish them.** Two honest paths:
1. Use the Instagram material for **direction, colour, framing and mood only**, and produce original imagery matched to it.

Either is defensible. The one thing that is not defensible is shipping a public, indexed site using their photos with no disclosure and no approval. **Decide this before Phase 1 builds around real files.** Yelp's 457 customer photos remain unusable under both paths.
